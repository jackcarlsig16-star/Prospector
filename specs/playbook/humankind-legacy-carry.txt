1. POSITIONING (business_profiles, generated 2026-08-19, rules edited 2026-08-21)
=====================================================================
Mission: make it easier and more fun to improve your life and your city - small, verified real-world actions that add
up to shared community progress. Tagline / motto: "Community starts with you."

Core problem solved: the gap between passive digital engagement and real-world community participation. Residents get a
structured, rewarding way to build better habits and support local businesses; local sponsors get verified, trackable
consumer behavior instead of impressions or clicks.

What it is: a free community wellness platform, live in Chicago (iOS and Android). Members complete weekly verified
challenges across five pillars - fitness, community, mindfulness, sustainability, learning - and earn points redeemable
for rewards and raffle entries from local business partners.

Products
- Sponsored Challenge: a 2-week featured brand challenge with verified completions and user-generated content from every
  submission; rewards or raffle entries can unlock on completion; average 50-150 completions per challenge (August 2026);
  comes with a monthly analytics report. Higher-consideration ask - pair it with a brand-specific challenge concept.
- Sponsored Reward / Raffle: a listing in the rotating rewards marketplace, redeemed only by members who have already
  earned points; tracks verified in-person or product redemptions; builds a loyalty loop. Lower-friction ask - the
  starting point for hesitant prospects.
- The HumanKind app itself (free to members).

Value props
- Every completion is verified - sponsors pay for real behavior, not impressions or clicks.
- Free for members, with real rewards from local Chicago businesses.
- Weekly challenges across five pillars keep engagement fresh.
- Sponsors get a monthly analytics report and measurable foot traffic, not vanity metrics.

Go-to-market (August 2026): grow the Chicago member base and the sponsor pipeline at the same time, with verified weekly
challenges as the connective tissue between members and local partners. Near-term priorities then: an influencer push,
in-person events with studio partners, and a possible national expansion through a distribution partnership with Do
Good media (see section 3b). First paying sponsors had given verbal commitment - the first signal that brands would pay
rather than only participate for free. Press at the time: a feature in CBS's Do Good Crew newsletter and a HALOTalks
guest slot being scheduled.

Industry line: community wellness / local sponsorship platform. Launch: on the App Store from April 2026 (one later
example says May - treat the month as history).

Outreach voice (outreach_rules, 2026-08-21) - the parts not already in skill v2
- Two sender registers exist: a traction-led, stats-forward founder voice (when there is no personal hook) and a
  personal-hook-led, warm, anecdotal voice (when a genuine connection to the brand exists). Business outreach is more
  polished; creator outreach is more casual and direct.
- Open with 1-2 sentences that show why this prospect was chosen, from real research. Name a concrete challenge concept
  tied to the brand that would not make sense for a generic sponsor - the challenge concept is the highest-leverage part
  of a cold business pitch.
- Intro names the 3 most relevant pillars only, never all five. The community link is its own standalone line.
- Cut a sentence before adding one. Plain hyphens, never em dashes.
- Creator outreach is one short, friendly, direct message: introduce HumanKind, explain the challenge / reward model in
  one compact paragraph, say HumanKind is looking for creators, ask whether they are interested and what their rates are.
  Never a sponsorship deck; the opportunity should be understandable in seconds.
- A generic contact form gets one form-sized message, never a five-touch sequence.

2. THE TWO-STEP SEQUENCE AND THE WINNING PATTERN (legacy project guidance, between 2026-08-14 and 2026-08-21)
=====================================================================
Objective then: get small Chicago businesses to pay to sponsor challenges; book as many meetings as possible and convert
them to paid challenge sponsors. Sponsoring challenges and reward redemption drives foot traffic, sales and awareness.
Target: small businesses in Chicago. Ask: 15 minutes on how HumanKind can drive foot traffic and / or sales.

What converted (Q3 strategy synthesis, 2026-08-19): the "challenge concept" framing with a 15-minute-call CTA.

Two-step sequence for larger retailers (NOT in skill v2 - carry): pushback from larger retailers prompted a shift to
two steps - lead with the challenge idea and request the call only after a positive reply. Smaller local businesses
can still get the single-touch concept + soft call ask.

Targeting note: geographic focus was being tightened around Logan Square and Humboldt Park small businesses, where a
"bring your own container" framing showed particular traction (sustainability pillar).

Winning pattern from a closed thread (Outbound Refined 2026 objective, 2026-08-21): a specific product-tie-in hook
(match the recipient's product to a concrete member behavior, not a generic "partnership"), a single soft CTA (15-minute
call), one polite bump if no reply, and case studies offered proactively the moment the prospect shows interest. That
sequence took a cold prospect to a booked meeting in three touches.

Distilled message shape (from the sent examples; the examples themselves are not carried)
- Brief casual greeting, sometimes first name only, sometimes the team name.
- One-sentence intro that always says "free community wellness platform / app" and anchors credibility with two stats:
  current member count and total challenge completions since launch (take both from hk_settings).
- A standalone "You can check out the community here!" line.
- The core: a highly personalized challenge concept tailored to the prospect's business, framed as an obvious fit ("a
  natural fit", "one of the easiest fits we've come across"), with a named challenge idea in quotes and a short list of
  reward options (discount, branded product, or raffle entry).
- One line on verification: every completion is verified, so the sponsor drives real behavior, not clicks and impressions.
- Close with a soft, low-pressure CTA - "Worth a 15-minute call to see if it makes sense?" - and a first-name sign-off.
- Tone: warm, direct, enthusiastic without being salesy; short, punchy sentences.
- The bump: a single polite nudge referencing the prior email and repeating the 15-minute ask, nothing else.

3b. Do Good partnership summary (2026-08-17) - the strategic logic and the pitch structure
- Do Good: a cause-driven media company with a podcast, newsletter and live events; about 500K monthly podcast
  listeners and 60K newsletter subscribers as of August 2026. On a call it offered to promote HumanKind as "the do good
  app" across both channels.
- Logic: HumanKind becomes a new revenue stream for Do Good and a new brand-sponsorship placement alongside its podcast,
  newsletter and events; a nationalized HumanKind powered by Do Good's audience would generate immediate revenue and
  growth for Do Good; longer term HumanKind backs into a local sponsor model city by city as member-density hotspots
  emerge from national usage data. Framing used: "huge audience through extension" that "powers the whole engine".
- One-pager structure (v2, external): The Fit (cause / audience alignment) -> The Opportunity (new revenue stream +
  unique brand placement) -> Proof It Works, Chicago Today (live since April 2026, sponsorship model, founding partners,
  challenge areas) -> Phased Roadmap (Chicago proof of concept -> Do Good partnership launch -> nationalized platform ->
  back into local markets; each phase split into value to Do Good vs value to HumanKind) -> The Ask: a low-risk pilot
  (one podcast episode, one newsletter placement, one live event, tracked attribution) before a full partnership.
- "The Engine" schematic: four components feeding a central Sponsorship Engine - Audience Extension (listeners,
  subscribers, live reach); New Revenue Line for Do Good (podcast, newsletter, live-event placements); Nationalized
  Platform (immediate revenue, immediate growth, national sponsor base); Local Reactivation (density hotspots,
  city-specific sponsors, hyper-local activation).
- Unresolved then: a partnership / distribution deal does not belong in the standard sponsor-tracker flow.
  (Participants, Do Good contact names and generated file names dropped.)

3c. Revenue-architecture draft (2026-08-18) - EXPLORATORY, not business logic
The source carries its own directive: these transactional percentages, B2B frameworks and partnership structures are
early product-development concepts, not finalized business logic or binding commitments. Carried as history.
- Core framing: a community-powered behavior-change engine and dual-sided marketplace that replaces passive social media
  engagement with verified real-world impact. Principles: verified action (photo / video proof, QR scans at partner
  locations, GPS check-ins - never self-reported); action-based funding (every verified action triggers a local reward
  and / or a micro-donation to a designated non-profit); ego-free reputation (verified community impact over clout).
- Flywheel: members complete verified actions -> earn local rewards and trigger non-profit donations -> sponsors gain
  foot traffic and ROI data -> platform value grows.
- Monetization channels sketched: sponsor SaaS plans (dashboard, custom challenge creation, ESG / CSR reporting; tiered
  from about 49 to 4,999+ per month); performance fees per verified action, QR check-in or redeemed reward; donation
  routing fee of 3% - 7% per transaction; an optional consumer tier at 5 per month; corporate wellness on a per-employee
  per-month B2B model aimed at corporate health and insurance risk pools.
- Payments stack idea: Stripe for subscriptions, per-action micro-charges and donation routing; Plaid plus ACH for larger
  B2B transactions; auto-routed funds 70-95% to the non-profit with a 3-7% processing fee.
- Pilot frameworks sketched: a fitness studio funding a 1-5 dollar food-pantry donation per verified workout with a free
  or discounted class as the member reward; a grocery "Serve & Save" (verified volunteer shift -> 10% coupon + a 5 dollar
  donation per action); a cafe "Volunteer & Sip" (volunteer at neighborhood non-profits -> free latte). Pitch value in
  each: clean ROI, clear foot-traffic tracking, measurable community goodwill.
