1. SUMMARY

HomeLover's current partners already cover all three employee income segments and both halves of the model: partners that bring employees in (distribution) and partners that deliver the service (data, agents, execution). The next step is scaling distribution. The fastest leverage is in places where one contract reaches many employers: perk marketplaces, PEOs, and benefits brokers. Enterprise consultants and HRIS marketplaces are the big long-cycle bets, so those relationships should start now.

Key takeaways:
• Employers are showing clear interest in housing benefits, but few employees have one today. In a 2024 survey, 25% of employers were considering housing benefits, and only 13% of employees currently get help renting or buying.
• Competitors are already locking up channels. Foyer sells through Nayya, and Multiply Mortgage sells through Sequoia's PEO network. Distribution partnerships are the land grab.
• HomeLover's edge over point solutions is that it covers renters, buyers and owners in one place, at $0 cost to the employer, with licensed professionals rather than coaching alone.

3. MARKET CONTEXT

• Employer demand: a January 2024 JW Surety Bonds survey (310 employers, 710 employees, reported by WorldatWork) found:
  – 25% of employers were considering housing benefits.
  – Employers already offering them planned about $6,201 per employee.
  – More than a quarter of employees said they would switch jobs for this benefit, and nearly a third would take it over a raise.
  – Only 13% of employees currently get employer help with housing.
  Note: this data is from 2024, so cite it with the date.
• Employer examples from 2026 coverage:
  – BNY offers $6,500 in down payment assistance to eligible US employees earning $100K or less.
  – Fannie Mae offers grants of up to $10,000.
  – Freddie Mac offers $15,000 for first-time buyers.
  – Amazon has a mortgage program with US Bank that counts stock and RSUs as income.
• Competitive moves:
  – Foyer (home savings) sells through the benefits navigation platform Nayya and is working with the National Association of Realtors to push for tax-advantaged homeownership savings accounts.
  – Multiply Mortgage is available through Sequoia's PEO network.
  – ORO Impact raised $3M in January 2026 (Slauson & Co., Northwestern Mutual Future Ventures) for a renter/buyer/owner housing benefit, with Framework as its homebuyer education partner.
• Policy:
  – Florida's proposed Homebuyer Workforce Tax Credit (HB 311 / SB 1672) would give employers a 100% tax credit for 1,000–5,000 toward a first-time buyer's down payment, for employees at or below 120% of area median income. It was moving through committees as of February 2026; final status needs to be confirmed.
  – A White House idea to allow 401(k) money for down payments was floated in January 2026 but has not materialized.


4. FUTURE LANDSCAPE: WHERE EMPLOYERS LOOK

When employers search "employer housing benefits," "employee housing assistance" or "employer assisted housing (EAH)," results fall into five categories. Each is a partnership opportunity.

4.1 Corporate perk platforms and marketplaces (top priority)
• Who: BenefitHub, PerkSpot, Access Development, EBG.
• What they are: employee perk and discount portals sold to employers, usually with single sign-on.
• Angle: be the featured housing module inside their directory, as a white-label or co-branded vendor. Most existing mortgage listings are generic rate discounts. HomeLover offers an end-to-end benefit: DPA search, rental rewards, closing credits and a licensed LO and agent.
• Economics: a listing or referral fee, or a revenue share, per closed transaction.

4.2 HR and benefits research and thought leadership
• Who: WorldatWork, National Housing Conference (NHC), Local Housing Solutions / Housing Solutions Lab, NAR.
• Angle: co-authored research on the ROI of employer-assisted housing; webinars and conference speaking slots; EAH implementation toolkits for HR leaders that feature HomeLover.

4.3 Tax, legal, accounting and consulting advisors
• Who: KPM CPAs, Mauldin & Jenkins, HoganTaylor, Liebert Cassidy Whitmore (LCW); consultants such as WTW and Aon.
• Angle: when these firms help employers structure down payment contributions or forgivable loans, HomeLover is the tech and execution layer. Co-host CPE webinars and set up referral relationships.

4.4 Direct EAH software vendors and point solutions (co-opetition)
• Who: ORO Impact, Benevate. (The original research also listed Crib Equity and Gerald. Gerald appears to be a cash-advance app, not an EAH vendor, and Crib Equity needs verification.)
• Angle: many of these handle only one piece (buying, coaching or education). HomeLover can offer fulfillment (licensed LOs, the agent network, closing credits) as a white-label backend, or partner where they lack a real estate transaction layer.

4.5 Government agencies and housing finance agencies
• Who: state HFAs, California HCD, local housing authorities, states with employer tax credits.
• Angle: stack state and municipal DPA with employer contributions for key workforce sectors (teachers, healthcare, municipal staff). HomeLover's DPA search already covers 2,600+ programs, which makes it the natural tool for stacking. Longer-term: public-private administration of public employee housing programs.


5. ADDITIONAL CHANNELS (NOT IN ORIGINAL SEARCH RESEARCH)

These don't rank for housing-benefit searches, but they control access to employers:

• PEOs (Justworks, Insperity, TriNet, Sequoia): one partnership reaches thousands of small and mid-size employers. Multiply Mortgage has already done this through Sequoia, which proves the channel works.
• Mid-market benefits brokers (Gallagher, Lockton, USI, NFP): the same motion as One Digital, repeated.
• HRIS and payroll marketplaces (ADP Marketplace, Workday, Rippling, Gusto): app-store style listings next to where employers run benefits. Long cycle, huge reach.
• Benefits administration and navigation (bswift, Businessolver, Nayya): where employees choose benefits during open enrollment. Foyer is already inside Nayya.
• Financial wellness and earned wage access (Brightside, Financial Finesse, DailyPay, Payactiv): already selling "financial stress" solutions to HR. Housing is the biggest financial stressor, so HomeLover is a natural add-on or referral.
• Membership organizations and unions: already a named HomeLover audience ("employers and membership organizations"). Member-benefit programs are a direct channel.
• Housing counseling agencies (Framework, HUD-approved counselors): homebuyer education partners that also add credibility with lenders and DPA programs.
• Anchor employers as case studies (hospitals, universities, school districts, cities): these organizations have run EAH programs for years and make strong proof points.
